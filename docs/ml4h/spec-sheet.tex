\title{AfyaCore: On-Device Speech-to-Structured Clinical Records for
Health Facilities Without Reliable Connectivity}

\begin{abstract}
AfyaCore turns a spoken consultation into a structured, encrypted clinical
record on the clinician's own phone, for primary-care facilities in sub-Saharan
Africa. Speech recognition (Whisper base) and de-identification (OpenMed PII)
run on the device; nothing leaves it unless the facility configures
synchronisation. A deterministic extractor turns the transcript into typed
fields, and nothing machine-derived enters the record until a clinician
confirms it beside its source phrase. It is technically validated, with field accuracy measured from speech through
the model files the phone runs, but has not been evaluated with clinicians.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Primary-care facilities across sub-Saharan Africa record consultations on
paper, written once and hard to retrieve at the next visit. Digitisation fails
on constraints of the setting: intermittent connectivity, low-end Android
hardware, and staff who cannot spend a consultation typing. AfyaCore is a capture tool for nurses, midwives and clinical officers at health
posts. The clinician dictates in French or English; on-device recognition
produces a transcript, a deterministic extractor parses vital signs, diagnosis
and prescriptions into typed fields, each shown with its source phrase to
confirm. It does not diagnose, triage or recommend treatment.

\section{Method}

\textbf{Architecture.} A single-page web application, optionally synchronised
to a self-hosted server. The initial load is 154\,kB gzipped, precached by a
service worker, and fully functional with the network off.

\textbf{Speech recognition.} Whisper \texttt{base} (72.6M parameters)
\citep{radford2022whisper}, ONNX, int8 (81\,MB), in a Web Worker through
\texttt{transformers.js} on WebAssembly. Without it, dictation is disabled
rather than sent to a cloud API.

\textbf{Extraction and review.} A deterministic, locale-aware rule system
rather than a language model, whose failure mode is a plausible wrong number
with nothing downstream to catch it. Each value carries a plausibility range,
is dropped if outside it, and is confirmed beside its source phrase; an
unconfirmed value is withheld from exports and aggregates.

\textbf{De-identification.} A deterministic stage removes every identifier the
device holds, then an optional on-device neural stage (OpenMed PII French
Clinical, 33M) \citep{openmed2025} catches those the roster never held. Records are
AES-GCM ciphertext at rest under a PIN-wrapped key.

Version 0.0.2, deployed publicly under MIT, has \emph{not} been piloted in a
facility; the evidence is technical validation. It carries 632 automated tests
and a fourteen-step offline walk through a real browser, transcribing both
languages before and after a disconnected reload and asserting identical
transcripts.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Measuring the pipeline, not its parts, found the defects.} An extractor
at 100\% and a recogniser with a published word error rate said nothing about
fields from speech until the two were scored together, which cost 33 to 40
points of F1. The recogniser writes a comma where a full stop was spoken, so a
diagnosis swallows the prescription after it, and spells drugs as it hears them
(\emph{par assez tamol} for \emph{parac\'etamol}). Each is now pinned by a test.

\textbf{A threshold nobody chose is not a control.} The flag for uncertain
values was \texttt{confidence} $<$ \texttt{0.8}, and the extractor gives every
diagnosis and chief complaint exactly 0.8, so the two fields a recogniser
mangles most could never be flagged; a reviewer reading flagged rows first
reached one error in five. Separately, a correction lands in an already-final
record, so an unconfirmed diagnosis reached a research export and a district
malaria count. Neither defect failed a test.

\textbf{The neural scrub is weakest where the tool is aimed.} All three off-roster identifiers it missed were Malagasy: a French model with an
English WordPiece vocabulary fragments Malagasy proper nouns. On real French
text it cost 2.4\% of gold clinical entities before a guard, including
\emph{lymphome malin non hodgkinien}, because Hodgkin is a surname.

\textbf{Hindsight and next.} An external review found five release blockers,
each a place where documentation flattered behaviour; the worst was dictation
streaming audio to the browser vendor under a claim of no third-party call. The
system was built to a technical standard before a clinician saw it, which is
the wrong order; next is a formative study at one facility.

\textbf{In-person demonstration.} The application installs from a URL and runs
offline with a synthetic workspace, dictation included, on attendees' own
devices with no network and no patient data. Its evaluation ships as a screen,
scoring a case against its reference on the device.
